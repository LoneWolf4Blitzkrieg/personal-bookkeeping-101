Personal Bookkeeping 101

Learn to follow every dollar of your paycheck on a real double-entry ledger page, by hand or on screen. Money is a system with rules, and once you know the rules there is nothing to fear.

A note from the author

Welcome. Before you begin Module 1, I want to ask one thing of you.

Do the checks and the final exam on your own. Please do not use AI for answers, and do not ask other people for answers. This course is free. There is no money reward, and there is no benefit in cheating. If you cheat, you only hurt yourself.

Alexander the Great did not conquer much of the world by cheating. Marcus Aurelius did not strengthen the Roman Empire by cheating. No one was ever granted real recognition of greatness by cheating. They earned it.

Be the person your ancestors imagined you to be. Take your time, reread when you need to, and make mistakes along the way. That is how you learn. Earn your certificate.

— Raul Bautista, author of Personal Bookkeeping 101

Start Module 1

New to working with money on paper? Take the five-minute money check first. If it shows any gaps, the money arithmetic refresher covers them.

Already keep books? An optional placement test can move you ahead: start at Module 5 (10 questions on Modules 1–4). After passing it, a second test can start you at Module 9 (10 questions on Modules 5–8). Each needs 80% or more.

What you will be able to do

  • Keep your own books on double-entry ledger paper, and prove they balance.
  • Budget with logic instead of emotion, by deciding where each dollar goes before you spend it.
  • Plan for the future and build an emergency fund.
  • Understand how the Federal Reserve, inflation and IRAs affect your money (add-on modules).

How the course works

  • 16 modules plus 3 add-on modules
  • 2.5 hours per module: 1 hour of lessons, 90 minutes of practice
  • About 8 weeks at two modules a week
  • Your own pace reread any step as often as you need

Every example is a page of ledger paper. Each module ends with a short check, and you move on once you have shown you understand it. If you need more time, take it; if you already know the early material, a placement test can move you ahead.
